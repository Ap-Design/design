Case Study 01Logistics & Freight Platform

Container
Management
Service

Control container moves, reduce handling costs, and ease empty runs and wait times.

Project
Description

Efficiency and cost savings accompany visibility, but containers are often as difficult to track as the goods they contain. Thanks to platform customers can consolidate container status updates and standardize the data from multiple sources.

With this insight, logistics teams can easily manage container movements—whether for day-to-day operations or strategic decisions. The overall results are lower demurrage, detention and lost container charges.

2

Track &
manage
containers

Container Management application has dedicated capabilities for shippers, trucking companies and ocean carriers.

  • Optimize street turns by re-using discharged import containers for export bookings
  • Request drop-off and pick-up changes online or automate the process for recurring moves
  • Improve container returns through online collaboration with multiple carriers
  • Use online container drop-off and pick-up location change requests
3

Design
challenge

Provide exceptional user experience for ocean shipping lines to negotiate prices for container moves with forwarders.

The company accepting the move can contact the shipping line directly through the UI. If they feel the proposed earnings are too low or don’t reflect the actual effort involved, they can suggest a different price for the move.

4

Problem
statement

I analyzed 4 months of Hotjar recordings and heatmaps, covering around 40k users. The analysis revealed that nearly 60% of users struggled to complete their tasks due to pricing-related issues.

Based on these findings, I developed 3 user personas and mapped their experiences through customer journey maps to better understand how they manage container moves, where they encounter friction, and what could be improved.

Solution

After the formation of the main questions, I’ve made another round of interviews with people and it helps me to create potential solution ideas through card sorting:

Testing
hypothesis

Before starting work on the backlog user stories, I began by testing a hypothesis.
Following initial interviews with shipping line representatives, I identified a potential usability issue: users might misinterpret a "0 price" and accidentally accept a job without realizing it was free of charge.
This insight prompted me to explore a smarter solution that would prevent misunderstandings and help users make more informed decisions.

12

Validation

I’ve come up with a quick low fidelity sketch and presented it to the team, and later prepared mockups and validated it with stakeholders that were very happy with a solution.

So the idea is:
When the shipping line does not want to set a price, they can leave it as “0 EUR” nor empty but enable the negotiate price feature.

In this scenario (negotiate = true and price = 0 || NULL), when the transporter selects Accept Job, alter the UI and require that they fill in a positive amount.

Conclusion

Achievements

Following a demo review with the team and an extended session with business stakeholders, my initial assumptions were validated, confirming that the chosen solution effectively met all business requirements. I requested that stakeholders test the functionality once implementation was complete. Shortly after deployment to the staging environment, heatmaps and session recordings confirmed that all user experience criteria were met, proving the interface to be both accessible and intuitive.

Areas for improvement

  • Notifications: Add a notification center (bell icon next to the user profile) to alert users of status changes.
  • Performance: Improve overall interface responsiveness.
  • Accessibility Testing: Conduct usability testing with a broader participant pool - including users with disabilities such as color blindness - to ensure the interface is comfortable and accessible for everyone.
